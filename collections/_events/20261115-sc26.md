**HPC Unites.**

**Chicago, IL, USA • 15–20 November 2026**

SC is the world's largest gathering of the HPC community, a week of workshops, tutorials, technical papers, panels, Birds of a Feather sessions and one of the biggest exhibitions in computing. The UK is once again out in force: **167 people at UK organisations are contributing to 91 items in the SC26 programme**, from a Gordon Bell finalist and an invited talk through to dozens of workshop papers, BoFs and tutorials. Our UK guide to the week is below.

## Visit the UK booths

There are four UK booths that you should definitely make time for on the exhibition floor this year.

### UK Universities (booth 4656)

After a successful debut at SC25, the UK Universities booth is back (listed in the SC26 exhibitor directory under UCL), giving universities from across the UK a shared space to showcase their research computing, highlight their successes and meet international partners. It is a great first stop to find out what is happening across the UK's research computing community and to meet the people behind it.

### EPCC, University of Edinburgh (booth 3455)

EPCC hosts the UK's national supercomputer, ARCHER2, and the Edinburgh International Data Facility, and has been a leading centre for HPC, data and AI since 1990. EPCC staff are busy across the week, including:

* **RISC-V and novel hardware:** the [International Workshop on RISC-V for HPC](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=17&sess=sess154) (Friday morning), with talks on a [community-developed Tenstorrent Tensix simulator](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=ws_risc111&sess=sess154) and [ET-SoC-1 vector performance](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=ws_risc108&sess=sess154), and BoFs on the RISC-V ecosystem and democratising AI accelerators for HPC (Thursday).
* **Sustainability:** the [Sustainable Supercomputing](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=17&sess=sess157) workshop (Friday morning).
* **Training, outreach and the future of the profession:** the [Modern High-Performance I/O](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=tut157&sess=sess182) tutorial (Monday), and BoFs on hands-on public outreach, on how HPC education must change when AI writes our code, and on AI-assisted engineering in HPC.
* **Art of HPC:** two visualisations on show in the exhibition all week.

### STFC (booth 4850)

The Science and Technology Facilities Council funds and runs large-scale UK research facilities. Its Scientific Computing Department and the Hartree Centre provide national HPC, data and AI services and help UK industry adopt advanced computing. STFC is also behind CoSeC, which supports collaborative computational research communities across UKRI. Look out for:

* The technical paper [Communication Avoiding Parallel Sketching via Sparse Subspace Embeddings](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=pap822&sess=sess198) (Tuesday) from the Rutherford Appleton Laboratory.
* Hartree Centre work on [QRMI as a unified interface for quantum–HPC integration](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=ws_sfwm112&sess=sess112) (Sunday).
* The [ScalAH'26 workshop](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=17&sess=sess122) on scalable algorithms for heterogeneous systems (Sunday afternoon), organised from the Hartree Centre.
* The [Thirteenth SC Workshop on Best Practices for HPC Training and Education](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=17&sess=sess150) (Friday morning).

### University of Bristol (booth 6804)

The Bristol Centre for Supercomputing (BriCS) runs Isambard-AI, the UK's flagship AI supercomputer, and Bristol has one of the busiest programmes of any UK organisation at SC26:

* The [Sovereign AI Supercomputing Cloud Workshop](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=17&sess=sess138) (Monday morning), plus workshop papers on running a fast-paced national AI resource, Slingshot as a first-class Kubernetes resource, and a threat model for sensitive-data workloads on multi-tenant AI supercomputers.
* A microarchitecture-level comparison of [NVIDIA Vera with Grace, Turin and Granite Rapids](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=ws_pmbsf156&sess=sess109) (Sunday).
* The panel [Brain Dump: HPC Experts Volunteer To Be Replaced by AI](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=pan107&sess=sess455) (Friday).
* BoFs on confidential supercomputing, OpenCHAMI, interoperable AI access portals, open-source and sovereign infrastructure, trusted research environments, and open-source architecture simulators.

Other UK exhibitors include [Quantum Motion](https://www.quantummotion.com/) (booth 5447), who also have a poster on their cloud-accessible silicon spin qubit processor.

## Programme highlights

Beyond the booths, a few UK contributions not to miss:

* **Gordon Bell finalist:** UKAEA's [Exascale Multiphysics Simulations for Liquid Metal Fusion Blankets](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=gb105&sess=sess197) is in the running for the ACM Gordon Bell Prize (Tuesday 10:52am).
* **Invited talk:** Peter Coveney (UCL) on [Real Science on Integrated Quantum-Classical Computers](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=inv103&sess=sess250) (Tuesday 1:30pm). He also appears on panels on medical digital twins and on the economics of hybrid HPC/ML.
* **Technical papers:** from the Universities of Leeds and Warwick on GPU inference, quantum circuit optimisation and graph neural network acceleration, alongside STFC's paper above.
* **Tutorials:** full-day tutorials on [Julia for HPC](https://sc26.conference-program.com/?post_type=page&p=16&id=tut134&sess=sess169) (UCL) and modern HPC I/O (EPCC) on Monday.
* **Weather and climate:** an invited workshop talk from the Met Office on moving from MPI+X to domain-specific abstractions for sustainable weather and climate software (Sunday).
